# Campus Customs — Design (Problem 10)

**Concept: "Elm City Editorial."** The site should feel like a printed collegiate catalogue from a real shop on Broadway, not a template. Ivory paper, Yale-blue ink, brass foil accents, hairline rules, numbered sections, and products presented as **hang tags**.

## What changed, and why it keeps shoppers around and buying

| Area | What we did | Why it helps shoppers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Product photos** | 73 of 102 catalogue photos sat on **black squares** (transparent PNGs flattened to black). The backend removes the flat background on first request (flood fill from the border, so garments are untouched), caches a transparent WebP in `data/products_cutout/`, and `/images/*` serves it, with the original as fallback. Originals are not modified. | Product photos are what sells. Every garment now sits cleanly on the same ivory "studio" background, so the grid looks curated rather than broken, and shoppers trust what they see. |
| **Color & type** | Palette: ivory `#f6f1e7`, Yale blue `#00356b`, midnight `#071d3a`, brass `#b08d57`. Fonts: **Fraunces** (editorial serif with italic accents), **Manrope** (clean UI text), **IBM Plex Mono** (tag-style labels and prices). | Reads as premium and local, which justifies $58–$98 price points. The mono labels make prices and sizes quick to scan. |
| **Hierarchy** | Numbered sections ("01 — The Collection"); one large serif headline per page with a blue italic phrase; eyebrow labels; generous whitespace; hairline rules. | Shoppers always know where they are and what to do next: one headline, one primary button. |
| **Product cards (hang tags)** | Photo on a soft studio gradient; a paper tag with punched hole, category + catalogue number, name, a 2-line blurb, **sizes (sold-out sizes struck through)** and a highlighted price. On hover the photo zooms, the tag tilts, and a "View the piece →" bar slides up. Catalogue names are tidied for display ("1 4 Zip" → "¼-Zip", "T Shirt" → "T-Shirt"). | Price and size availability before the click mean fewer dead ends and more confident clicks. The hover tilt makes browsing feel tactile, so shoppers look at more products. |
| **Home page** | Hero: "Dressed for *the Elm City.*" plus three real products hanging on brass strings (live names and prices from the API) and our crest. Live stats strip (styles, silhouettes, XS–XXL, pieces ready). "Shop by silhouette" tiles that link straight into filtered lists (`/products?cat=hoodie`). "The *Game*." rivalry band. Editor's picks. A concierge demo. | It shows real products in the first second and gives three fast paths to buying: a category, a featured piece, or the Concierge. The rivalry band gives a seasonal reason to buy now. |
| **Product page** | A framed photo with **cursor-follow zoom**; serif name; price tag; "In stock at 57 Broadway" pill; **colorway swatches**; size buttons (sold-out ones hatched and struck through); an availability table with green, amber and empty dots; a prominent **"Ask the Concierge about this"** card. | Shoppers can inspect prints up close, see stock by size at a glance, and get questions answered without leaving the page. Those are the last steps before a purchase decision. |
| **Products page** | Editorial header, a sticky frosted filter panel (search, size, sort, category chips), and loading skeletons instead of a blank page. Concierge results get a "Curated by your Concierge" banner. | Filters stay in reach while scrolling a long list. Skeletons make the page feel fast, so fewer shoppers bounce while it loads. |
| **Chat ("The Concierge")** | A pill launcher with our crest ("Ask the Concierge"), a panel that springs in, a navy header with an online dot, ivory/white bubbles with brass list markers, **animated typing dots**, product cards with snap-scroll, and brass quick-reply chips. | A named, polished assistant feels like a staff member rather than a widget, so shoppers use it more. The chips and cards turn answers into clicks toward products. |
| **Motion** | Scrolling announcement ticker; page fade-in on every route; scroll-reveal with staggered cards; swaying hang tags; button sheen; nav underline grows from the left; frosted nav on scroll. All of it is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`. | Small, consistent motion makes the shop feel alive and invites scrolling, without slowing anyone down or bothering motion-sensitive users. |
| **Brand marks** | An original **CC crest** (shield, interlocked italic CC, brass elm leaf), deliberately not a Yale mark; matching favicon; footer with an outlined "Boola." wordmark and quick links to categories. | A consistent identity builds recognition and trust; the footer category links give one more path back into the catalogue. |
| **Auth pages** | Split layout: a midnight panel listing the member benefits (saved chat, greeting by name, hashed passwords) next to an underlined-field form. | Gives shoppers a reason to create an account. Logged-in shoppers get chat memory, which brings them back. |
| **Accessibility** | Skip link, visible brass focus rings, `aria` labels on chips, size buttons (`aria-pressed`) and the typing indicator; color is never the only signal (struck-through sizes plus text labels). | More shoppers can complete a purchase. |
